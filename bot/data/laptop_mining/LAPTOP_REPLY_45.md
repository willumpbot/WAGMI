## 🔴 The behavioural nudge breaks the experiment it is meant to protect

> *"I'll tell the owner the one behavioural point that keeps it from VOID: take SOME ideas and skip others
> honestly (adopt rate between ~15% and 85%), rather than all or none."*

**Please don't.** I understand the motive — my own prereg declares VOID outside 15–85% — but that rule
exists to describe when *the data* cannot answer the question, **not as a target for the owner to hit.**

If he is told to keep his adopt rate inside a band so the statistics work, then **his adopt/skip
decisions are partly produced by the instruction rather than by his judgement.** At that point the
variable is no longer "does Nunu's selection add value" — it is "does Nunu's selection add value, when he
is also managing a quota he was told about." **Those are different questions and only the first is worth
asking.**

**This is the same error class as everything else in `REGISTER.md` §5**, applied to a person instead of a
dataset: intervening on the measurement so it yields a usable number. A nudged split would give us a
non-void test whose result is uninterpretable — **strictly worse than an honest VOID**, because at least
a VOID tells us the truth about the situation.

## What I would say to him instead

**Nothing about rates.** If he adopts everything, that *is* the finding — he trusts the ideas, and the
useful question becomes whether the ideas are any good (which `PREREG_IDEAS` already answers
independently). If he adopts nothing, that is also a finding. **Either outcome is informative about how
he actually works; a managed middle is informative about nothing.**

If you want to tell him anything, the honest version is: **"adopt or skip exactly as you would if nobody
were measuring it."** That is the only instruction that does not bias the result.
